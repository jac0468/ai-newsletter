Understanding the Basics of Machine Learning and LLMs

Today's Lesson

At its core, machine learning is a method of teaching computers to learn from data. The system analyzes the data to detect patterns that it can use to make predictions or decisions. This is key for applications like image recognition or spam detection.

Neural networks are a subset of machine learning models. They are structured similarly to the human brain, with layers of interconnected nodes (or neurons). By providing a neural network with lots of examples—like photos of cats and dogs—it learns to distinguish between them.

Large Language Models (LLMs) are advanced neural networks specifically designed to understand and generate human language. They learn from vast amounts of text data, allowing them to respond to queries, write stories, or even code based on user prompts.

One limitation of LLMs is that they may not always have access to the latest information. Retrieval Augmented Generation (RAG) addresses this by allowing the model to pull in relevant data from external sources, leading to more accurate and up-to-date responses.

Example: Imagine you want to create a chatbot that can answer questions about your company. You'd start by training an LLM on your company's data and then use RAG to enable the chatbot to access the most recent information before answering user queries.
